How to use this guide

A chart is an argument, not a decoration. It tells the reader what to compare, what changed, and what matters most in a dataset before they process a single number. Pick the wrong chart and the argument gets lost, or worse, it tells the wrong story entirely.

This guide covers 15 chart types across three families: comparison charts (how things stack up against each other), change charts (how something moved over time), and relationship charts (how two or more variables interact). Each entry names the data shape the chart needs, when it is the right pick, when it misleads, a concrete example, and one tip for using it well.

The bonus section at the end covers 8 chart mistakes that quietly make correct data look wrong: the kind of thing that gets caught in a meeting, not before it.
