Your headline is the one line working 24 hours a day without you

Your LinkedIn headline appears in search results, comment sections, connection requests, and DMs, often before anyone has read a single word of your profile. It is the line that answers "should I click this person?" in under two seconds. And most people waste it on a job title.

In one audit of 400 LinkedIn profiles across B2B industries, 71% used only their current role and company name as their headline. That is the equivalent of a name badge at a conference that says only your name. A headline should tell someone what you do, who you do it for, and what changes because of it, all in 220 characters or fewer.

These 20 formulas are organised by goal: positioning yourself, demonstrating authority, naming your audience, and telling a story. Pick the one that fits your situation, fill in the brackets, and test it for two weeks. The right headline doesn't just describe you. It attracts the right people to you before you've said a word.
